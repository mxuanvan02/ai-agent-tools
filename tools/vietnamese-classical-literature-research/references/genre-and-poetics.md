# Thể loại và thi pháp (genre and poetics)

How to classify a work and how to analyse its form. Every Han string below is in
`han-nom-keyword-glossary.md` with its verification status; the analytical
statements are sourced to the corpus pages fetched while building this skill.

## Step 1 — classify on three independent axes

A single label hides the useful distinctions. Ask all three:

1. **Script** — chữ Hán (`漢字`) or chữ Nôm (`喃`)? This determines which
   scholarship and which catalogues apply. Many works exist in both: a Hán
   original with a Nôm rendering (`Chinh phụ ngâm` → `征婦吟曲`) is two objects
   with two histories.
2. **Function** — what was it for? A `誥` (cáo) is a state proclamation, a `記`
   (ký) is a record, a `賦` (phú) is display prose-poetry, a `銘` (minh) is an
   inscription. Function explains form far better than "genre" does.
3. **Form** — verse pattern, parallel structure, length, presence of a
   commentary apparatus (`評` lời bình, `評點` bình điểm, `序跋` tựa bạt).

Example: `傳奇漫錄` is Hán (1), narrative fiction in the `傳奇` mode (2),
prose mixed with biền văn and poems, each story closed by a lời bình (3). Each
axis generates different searches.

## Step 2 — narrative modes

| Mode | 漢字 | What it is | Query note |
|---|---|---|---|
| chí quái | `志怪` | Records of the strange; short, anecdotal, closer to note-taking | Chinese-language scholarship on the mode |
| truyền kỳ | `傳奇` | "Transmitting the marvellous"; developed narrative, characterisation, deliberate artifice | Retrieves comparative studies of the form across East Asia |
| chương hồi | `章回小說` | Chapter-form novel | `Hoàng Lê nhất thống chí` is usually read in this frame |
| bút ký | `筆記小說` | Notebook fiction; informal, accumulated | The right frame for `Vũ trung tùy bút`, which its author called "tùy bút" but which is not the modern essay |

The `志怪` → `傳奇` distinction is load-bearing for `Truyền kỳ mạn lục`: whether
Nguyễn Dữ collects and retells (sưu tập) or creates (sáng tác) is exactly the
question that separates a compilation from a work of literature, and Vietnamese
scholarship argues it explicitly (Bùi Duy Tân's position, recorded in the fetched
corpus, is that it is genuine literary creation, not a compilation like
`嶺南摭怪`). That argument is a research direction, not a footnote.

## Step 3 — functional prose genres

| Vietnamese | 漢字 | Register |
|---|---|---|
| chiếu | `詔` | imperial edict |
| cáo | `誥` | proclamation to the realm |
| biểu | `表` | memorial to the throne |
| hịch | `檄` | call to arms |
| phú | `賦` | rhapsody; display, often rhymed |
| minh | `銘` | inscription, usually on stone or bronze |
| ký | `記` | record; also the suffix in narrative titles |
| tự bạt | `序跋` | prefaces and postfaces — where dating and attribution evidence lives |
| bình điểm / lời bình | `評點` / `評` | interlinear and end commentary |

Two consequences worth acting on:

- **`序跋` is where the evidence is.** Prefaces name compilers, dates, patrons and
  earlier editions. `Truyền kỳ mạn lục` is dated partly from Hà Thiện Hán's 1547
  preface. If a dating or authorship question is open, read the prefaces first.
- **`評` is a primary source, not decoration.** The lời bình at the end of each
  story in `傳奇漫錄` states a moral and literary judgement; treating it as
  paratext loses the author's own reading of his work.

## Parallel prose (biền ngẫu)

`駢偶` / `駢文` / `駢體文` — prose built on paired clauses. From the fetched
Chinese corpus: it favours four- and six-character clauses (四六句法, hence the
alternate name 四六文), requires tonal balancing between the paired halves, and
when rhymed is called `駢賦`. The Vietnamese term in practice is **biền ngẫu** or
**biền văn**; both occur in the corpus, and `Bạch Đằng giang phú` is described as
phú without rhyme written in biền ngẫu style.

Why it matters analytically: in biền ngẫu the **pair** is the unit of meaning.
A translation or a paraphrase that flattens the pair into a single sentence
destroys the argument's rhythm and often its logic — the two halves contrast,
complete, or escalate. Read the two halves against each other before reading the
sentence.

## Regulated verse (luật thi)

Canonical Vietnamese usage (from the fetched Vietnamese corpus): **thất ngôn bát
cú** — eight lines of seven characters — is treated as the standard form, with
variants **thất ngôn tứ tuyệt** (four lines of seven), **ngũ ngôn tứ tuyệt** and
**ngũ ngôn bát cú**. The corpus also notes that in later practice the strictness
of the rules decayed considerably.

**Tones.** `平仄` classifies the four traditional tones 平上去入: 上, 去, 入 are
**trắc** (oblique); 陰平 and 陽平 are **bằng** (level). The tonal pattern of a
line is its 格律.

**The flexibility rule.** Recorded verbatim in the corpus as a mnemonic:
`一三五不論，二四六分明` — in seven-character lines the tones of positions 1, 3, 5
are free, while 2, 4, 6 are strict. This is the rule that makes "the poem breaks
the pattern" claims usually wrong: check positions 2-4-6 before declaring a
violation.

**Rhyme.** `押韻` (also written 壓韻) creates the recited musicality. In
regulated verse the rhyme falls on the level tone at line ends; the Vietnamese
corpus notates trắc rhymes with "T" and leaves unregulated positions blank, and
gives the pattern for the 2-4-6-7 positions.

**Parallelism.** `對仗` types, from the fetched corpus:

| Type | 漢字 | Property |
|---|---|---|
| công đối | `工對` | strict: same word class and register in both halves |
| khoan đối | `寬對` | relaxed: only word class or sense needs to match |
| tự đối | `自對` | the parallelism is inside a single line (e.g. 花開 / 花謝) |
| cách cú đối | `隔句對` (also `扇對`, `雙句對`) | line 1 pairs with line 3, line 2 with line 4 |
| trường ngẫu đối | `長偶對` | odd lines pair with odd, even with even, at least three sets |

The Vietnamese corpus frames the same material as two principles, **đối âm**
(tonal) and **đối ý** (semantic). Naming which kind of parallelism you found is
the difference between an observation and an analysis.

## Vietnamese Nôm verse forms

These are Vietnamese-side terms; they do **not** have a standard Han form in the
verified table, so search them in Vietnamese only.

- **lục bát** (6–8) — the national metre. `Truyện Kiều` 3,254 lines,
  `天南語錄` 4,068 couplets (= 8,136 lines, recorded as the longest lục bát work),
  `蓼雲仙傳`, `二度梅` 2,826 lines.
- **song thất lục bát** (7–7–6–8) — the metre of the ngâm khúc:
  `征婦吟曲`, `宮怨吟`, `天南明鑑`.
- **ngâm khúc** (lament) and **truyện thơ Nôm** (verse narrative) — the two
  dominant long Nôm forms. Searching these genre words retrieves survey
  scholarship that title searches miss.
- **hát nói** — a late, performance-bound form; closer to the modern period.

Pitfall: **do not apply luật thi rules to lục bát.** Lục bát has its own tone
scheme and its own rhyme linkage (the 6th syllable of the bát line rhymes with
the 6th of the next lục line). Analysing it with regulated-verse criteria
produces confident nonsense.

## Allusion (điển cố)

`典故` is the standard term; `用典` is the practice. Reading a classical poem
without resolving its allusions yields paraphrase, not interpretation, because
the allusion usually carries the argument: it imports a whole prior situation and
invites a comparison.

Working method:
1. Collect the unresolvable names, places and quoted phrases in a passage.
2. Look each up in a Han-Nom dictionary (`hvdic.thivien.net` is scriptable) and
   in the Chinese classics corpus (`ctext.org`, browser-only).
3. Record for each: the source text, the situation there, and what the comparison
   asserts here.
4. Distinguish **allusion** (imports a source) from **conventional imagery**
   (a shared vocabulary that imports nothing specific). Claiming an allusion that
   is only convention is a common over-reading.

## The examination essay (bát cổ)

`八股文` — the eight-legged essay of the examination system. Its parts, from the
fetched corpus: `破題` (two sentences opening the topic), `承題` (four or five
sentences developing it), `起講`, then `起股`, `中股`, `後股`, `束股`.

Relevance beyond the exam hall: generations of Vietnamese literates were trained
to think in this architecture — state, develop, pair, balance, close. Its traces
show up in argumentative prose that was never an examination answer. When a text's
structure looks rigidly bipartite for no narrative reason, this training is a
candidate explanation worth testing rather than assuming.

## Close-reading record (what to write down)

For each passage you analyse, capturing these prevents the "I read it and it was
good" report:

1. Script and language of the witness you are reading (Hán original, Nôm
   rendering, modern phiên âm, translation — and which one).
2. Genre and function (axes 1–3 above).
3. Form: line lengths, tone pattern at positions 2-4-6, rhyme words, type of
   parallelism found.
4. Allusions resolved, with sources.
5. Commentary apparatus present in the witness (tựa, bạt, bình, chú).
6. Variants between witnesses, if you have more than one.

Items 1 and 5 are what most analyses omit, and both change the conclusions.

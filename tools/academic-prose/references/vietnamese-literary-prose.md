# Vietnamese Prose About Literature and Comparison

Load when the deliverable is Vietnamese academic prose whose subject is literature,
especially a comparison of two literary works. Literary Vietnamese fails differently
from data Vietnamese: the punctuation scan returns zero, the calque corpus check returns
clean, and the prose still reads as a translation of English literary criticism. The
register carries its own idiom, its own hedge inventory, and its own false positives.

Title construction is not covered here; see
[title-design-method](../../document-skills/hujos-vi-manuscript-conventions/references/title-design-method.md)
for the four-slot method, the build order, and the failure signature behind each
rejection. Apply it before drafting, not after the author rejects a candidate.

Where that companion reference is not shipped alongside this one, the three-question
standard below is the self-contained version:

1. **Does it name a concrete image or event in the text?** A tree, a meal, a look,
   a refusal. Not a concept. A title named after its theory label instead of its
   finding fails here.
2. **Does it state what the paper claims about that image?** The claim, not the
   topic. A title that stops at a shared theme fails here.
3. **Can a reader who has not seen the abstract say what the paper argues about
   these two works?** A title narrower or wider than the argument fails here.

A colon or a spaced hyphen inside a Vietnamese title is a symptom, not the disease.
Both appear when the writer had two ideas and no syntax to join them: the first half
names the topic, the second supplies the claim, because the writer could not compress
both into one phrase — which is question 2 failing. The repair is compression, not
punctuation substitution: find the phrase that carries image and claim together, then
let prepositions (`trong`, `của`, `và`) do the joining work a colon would do in
English.

## 0. The standard, not the word list

A word list catches the sentences already seen. A test catches the ones not yet
written. When the author asks for a standard (`xây dựng cái chuẩn, áp dụng được
trong mọi trường hợp`), a longer calque table is the wrong answer: the same
structural fault reappears under fresh surface forms, and every substitution
passes while the prose still reads translated.

The fault has one cause. Translation preserves the source's information
architecture, and English criticism is built on abstraction-first sentences
(`The difference lies in the fact that...`, `What is at stake here is...`).
Rendered clause by clause these become Vietnamese whose subject is an abstraction
and whose predicate is a relation: grammatical, idiomatic in vocabulary, hollow.

§1 catalogues what has already been seen. The tests below state what must be
true, and they apply to any sentence, including one no table lists.

### Sentence tests

| # | Ask | Fails when | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | What is the main verb, and what observable action does it name? | the verb is `là`, `nằm ở`, `thuộc về`, `liên quan đến`, `thể hiện`, `phản ánh` over an abstract noun | recover the event the sentence was gesturing at; if none comes to mind the sentence has no content, so delete it |
| T2 | Who does this? | the answer is `điều này`, `việc ấy`, `quá trình`, `cách`, or nothing at all | name the actor, or recast with the affected entity as subject and the agent in a `bởi`/`do` phrase |
| T3 | Delete this clause. What does the sentence lose? | nothing | delete it. A clause that survives deletion was never carrying a claim. This catches every framing move: announcing a point, remarking that something is easy to miss, noting that a detail matters |
| T4 | Translate it into English. Does the English read naturally? | the English comes out fluent, with the same clause order and the same connectives | the Vietnamese was built on an English frame; rebuild in Vietnamese order, or split one English sentence into two |
| T5 | Read it aloud. Where do you run out of air? | it cannot be delivered in one breath, or the grammar must be searched for mid-clause | put the full stop at the joint where the logical relation changes, not where the noun phrase ends |
| T6 | Is any content word (4+ chars) repeated within 5 words? | the repetition is accidental — not emphasis, anaphora, or a locked technical term | restructure so the concept appears once and governs both predicates, or delete the redundant occurrence. **Never reach for a synonym** — synonym substitution over an empty structure produces inflated prose and manufactures a distinction the argument does not make (see `ai-pattern-vietnamese.md` §5). Exception: locked terms (`chức năng tác giả`, `chuỗi thể chế`) repeat by design when each occurrence carries new information |
| T7 | What noun does this pronoun (`nó`, `họ`, `điều này`) refer to? | the referent is more than one clause back, or two nouns could both fit | repeat the noun, or recast so the pronoun is unnecessary. Vietnamese tolerates zero-pronoun more than English, but only when the referent is unambiguous |
| T8 | Is this sentence listing three or more items? | items are separated by semicolons and each contains internal commas or clauses | convert to numbered sentences (`Thứ nhất,... Thứ hai,...`) or a bulleted list. Semicolons are licensed only for short parallel phrases without internal structure |

T1's repair is not "use a stronger verb". A stronger verb over an empty
proposition produces inflated prose. The repair is to recover the event.

### Voice tests

Empty prose passes T1–T5 as soon as its verbs are concrete. Literature needs a
second standard, applied per paragraph.

| # | Ask | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V1 | Does the paragraph open on something that happens in the text? | it opens by summarising the plot or the character's biography in order |
| V2 | Does any sentence land? | every sentence runs 20–35 words, so nothing is emphasised |
| V3 | Lift one sentence out. Does it still say something? | no sentence survives outside its neighbours, because each only modifies the previous |
| V4 | Where did this verb come from, the work or the framework? | the verbs are `thể hiện`, `phản ánh`, `khẳng định`, `đặt vấn đề`, `gợi mở` |
| V5 | Where did this image come from? | the figures are `cấu trúc`, `mô hình`, `trục`, `tầng` rather than the work's own material |
| V6 | Does this paragraph move the reader, or tell them it is moving? | the work is done by adjectives: `ám ảnh`, `đau đớn`, `bi thảm`, `lay động` |
| V7 | Is this sentence reading or retelling? | it narrates events in order |
| V8 | Does the paragraph close on weight? | it closes by announcing what comes next (`Tiếp theo, bài viết sẽ ...`) |

**V1 is the highest-value repair and the one an author notices first.** An opening
that summarises the plot is information the reader could get from the blurb, and it
establishes nothing about why the work matters. Repair: take the single image or
action that carries the work's argument, render it in the work's own terms across
two or three sentences, then state what it shows. A comparative opening pairs two
such scenes and lets the contrast carry the thesis; it does not announce that a
comparison is coming.

V2 is structural, not cosmetic. The long sentence builds and the short sentence
lands; a paragraph of uniform medium sentences has no emphasis anywhere, so nothing
is memorable. Close a built claim with six words or fewer that state the
consequence — the short sentence must carry a claim, not a transition.

V6 is the one literary agents violate most often: the adjective claims an effect
the prose has not produced. Delete the adjective and keep the detail that produced
it; if no detail produced it, the emotion was asserted and the sentence goes.

### Punctuation as structural commitment

Every punctuation mark that alters clause boundaries — question marks, colons,
semicolons, em dashes — is a structural commitment, not a stylistic choice. The
mark commits the writer to a specific discourse relation, and the surrounding
prose must fulfil that relation.

- **Question marks** commit to an interrogative structure: the following
  discourse must answer the question. A question opening a section and resolved
  by its claims is licensed; a question mid-argument breaks the claim–evidence
  chain and signals the writer thinking aloud; a question in the conclusion
  signals the writer has not decided.
- **Colons** commit to an explanatory relation: what follows must elaborate,
  exemplify, or enumerate what precedes. A colon followed by an independent
  clause that does not explain the preceding one is a structural fault.
- **Em dashes** commit to a parenthetical or contrastive insertion: the material
  between dashes must be removable without destroying the main clause's
  grammaticality. If the dash material is essential to the argument, it belongs
  in the main clause.

Repair by naming the relation the mark commits to and checking the prose
fulfils it. If it does not, either restructure the prose or downgrade to a mark
that commits to a weaker relation (comma, full stop).

## 1. Calques from English literary criticism

English criticism has a characteristic idiom. Translated clause by clause it yields
Vietnamese that is grammatical, invisible to a watched-word scan, and wrong.

| Calque | English source | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `nằm ở chỗ ...` (opening a clause) | lies in the fact that | state the proposition: `Khác biệt là ai có quyền xác nhận hình hài mới` |
| `đóng gói chính xác lựa chọn ấy` | packages the choice | `nói đúng lựa chọn ấy` |
| `cái đứng ấy làm mọi người lúng túng` | that standing makes everyone uneasy | `tư thế ấy làm mọi người lúng túng` |
| `ở tầng sâu nhất` | at the deepest level | name the meeting point, or delete |
| `đặt ra một điểm mà` | raises a point that | `Từ đây mở ra một điểm` |
| `chính chỗ X là chỗ Y` | the very place where | `chính nơi X là nơi Y` |
| `rất nặng` (about a quotation) | weighs heavily | `đặt vào đây thì chạm` |
| `điều kiện cần thêm nằm ở chỗ` | the condition to add is that | `điều kiện cần thêm là` |
| `bị làm dở` | half-done | `dở dang` |
| `như gợi ra` | as suggesting | `như hàm ý` |
| `hiện ra rõ hơn` | becomes clearer | `rõ hơn` |

`nằm ở chỗ` is the highest-frequency tell in translated Vietnamese criticism. A
10.000-word manuscript carrying it nine times reads as a translation even though no
single sentence is ungrammatical. Cap it at one per manuscript, or convert every
instance into a direct proposition — the repair is always shorter than the calque.

### Sweep headings and cross-references, not only body prose

Section titles are where a calque survives longest: written first, re-read least, and a
heading tolerates a relation where a sentence would not. `3.3. Nguyên nhân, và chỗ cây
thực sự nổi trội` and `6.3. Cái nhìn cuối cùng, và chỗ hành vi phản kháng đổi chủ` both
carry the English `where ...` frame. Rewrite as a noun or verb phrase: `và nơi cây thực
sự nổi trội`, `và điều khác nhau giữa chúng`. After any content pass, grep the heading
lines themselves — `^[0-9]+(\.[0-9]+)?\.  ` and `^#{1,4} ` — for `chỗ`, `nằm ở`, `:` and
` - `. A body sweep never reaches them, so a manuscript can read clean in every paragraph
and still open each section with a translation.

### Repair one language, not both

A calque list repairs Vietnamese and says nothing about the English source, where the
same construction is often idiomatic: `the difference lies in who has the authority` is
good English, so a mirrored fix on the English version degrades it. Apply the repair to
the language that fails, and check the other for what actually breaks there — in English
it is repetition (`the place where both are true is the place where the novel asks`),
not relation-verbs. Two versions of one paper diverge in their defects; a symmetric edit
pass fixes one and breaks the other.

## 2. `và` chains where Vietnamese wants a full stop

English criticism licenses periodic sentences. Vietnamese academic prose does not carry
them well, and a model reproducing the English sentence shape emits `..., và ..., và ...`
that a Vietnamese reader experiences as breathless. The tell is not the word `và` but
the number of independent predications in one sentence.

- Before: `Vì cô không thành, hành vi ấy phải rời khỏi cô và đi vào một người khác, và người ấy chỉ có một cái nhìn.`
- After: `Vì cô không thành, hành vi ấy phải rời khỏi cô để đi vào một người khác. Người ấy chỉ có một cái nhìn.`

- Before: `Cô gầy rạc đi, bị cha tát và nhét thịt vào miệng trong một bữa cơm gia đình, bị chồng cưỡng hiếp, bị đưa vào bệnh viện tâm thần, và ở phần cuối đứng chổng ngược trong hành lang, tuyên bố rằng rễ đang mọc ra từ lòng bàn tay.`
- After: `Cô gầy rạc đi. Trong một bữa cơm gia đình, cha cô tát cô rồi nhét thịt vào miệng cô. Người chồng cưỡng hiếp cô. Cô bị đưa vào bệnh viện tâm thần. Đến phần cuối, cô đứng chổng ngược trong hành lang và tuyên bố rằng rễ đang mọc ra từ lòng bàn tay.`

A sentence over 55 words in Vietnamese body prose is a revision target regardless of how
well built it is. Split at the causal joint, not at the nearest comma. Check with a
word count rather than by ear — long sentences feel normal to the agent that wrote them.

## 3. State the tertium comparationis in the introduction

A comparative-literature manuscript must name the axis of comparison in one sentence
before any analysis, and the axis is not the topic.

- Topic (insufficient): `cả hai đều nói về cơ thể nữ`
- Axis (sufficient): `hình thức kể quyết định khả năng rời đi`

When the axis is missing, reviewers read the pairing as arbitrary — two texts from
different millennia, different languages, different genres — and they read any
quantitative appendix as decoration. The same 64-case survey becomes load-bearing only
after the axis is stated, because the numbers then answer a question the paper has
already asked. If the appendix survives a reviewer challenge, that is the test: could
the survey have come out the other way and forced a different thesis? If no, cut it.

### The axis must be something the era determines

When the two works come from different periods, the choice of axis decides whether the
comparison is a historical argument or an arbitrary pairing. An axis drawn from
individual choice — who narrates, who decides, who speaks — invites the objection that
the two authors are simply different people, and the comparison collapses into parallel
description of two texts.

The axis that survives is a constraint the period imposes on the form: what the genre of
that age is obliged to accomplish. State it as a task, then show the task forces the
textual feature under study. A genre that must explain the world has to end each episode
at a form one can point to; a genre with no such obligation is free to leave the episode
unfinished. The difference then follows from the form rather than from the author's
temperament, and it stands without any claim about influence.

Test the axis by asking what would falsify it. If the answer is `nothing, because the
texts simply differ`, the axis restates the difference. If the answer is `a work of the
same genre that violates the constraint`, the axis is doing work.

Before committing, list the architectures the material supports — genre constraint,
single-scene close reading, rhetorical figure, ontology of the body, reception history —
and pick by which one the evidence can actually carry. Each needs different evidence: a
genre argument needs the form's obligation, a scene argument needs two passages read at
full resolution, a reception argument needs a documented chain. Choosing the architecture
is choosing what must be proved, so it precedes drafting; revisiting it later means
rewriting the abstract, the introduction and the conclusion together, not patching one.

When the axis moves, the evidence's role moves with it. A survey that was the backbone
under the old axis becomes corroboration under the new one: it tests one consequence of
the claim instead of establishing the claim. Demote it in the text — say what it
corroborates — rather than leaving a section that still argues from the numbers.

Guard the survey against confirmation bias explicitly. Coding performed by one author
with no second coder is a limitation to state, not to hide, and the sensitivity
analysis belongs in the paper rather than in a footnote: report which borderline cases
flip the result, and say that the finding is suggestive rather than established.

### Derived tables carry two attributions

A table built by reclassifying a co-author's coding sheet has two authors of different
kinds, and the source line must name both or it misattributes the work. `Nguồn: X lập` is
false when X compiled the cases and the present paper assigned them to new categories.
Write `Bài viết phân loại lại từ bảng mã hóa của X ở Phụ lục A`. The same distinction
applies to any table computed from someone else's raw data: the data has an owner and the
classification has an owner, and a reviewer who checks will find the difference.

Cutting one corpus along two different axes produces two tables whose subtotals disagree,
and the disagreement reads as an arithmetic error unless it is explained where the second
table appears. Say which axis each table uses, give the reason a case can sit in different
groups under each, and name the specific cases that move. A reader who can reconcile the
two counts by hand trusts both; a reader who cannot suspects the whole survey.

Verify a source file before coding from it. A document the author sends may not contain
the dataset they believe it does: count the tables and the words before assuming, because
a prose essay citing encyclopaedic sources is not a coding sheet, and rebuilding a table
from the wrong file produces numbers nobody can reproduce.

### A theoretical frame carries a referent discipline — relabelling is not application

A frame borrowed from philosophy names a specific object inside a specific discipline.
When the paper uses the frame's vocabulary for a neighbouring object, every sentence
stays grammatical and the argument becomes a misapplication that a specialist referee
detects immediately.

Measured case: a comparative manuscript applied Foucault's *author function* to two
Vietnamese novels and then analysed **narrative voice** — who narrates, whether the
narrator witnessed the events, whether the narrator inherited the tale. All of that is
Genette's territory: voice and focalization, a property *inside* the text. Foucault's
author function is a relation *outside* the text — the institutional, legal, and
classificatory apparatus that makes a discourse bear a name. The manuscript named the
right theorist on every page and analysed the wrong object.

The diagnostic question: **where does the concept live?** If the answer is "in the
text's own arrangement of who speaks", the frame is narratology. If the answer is "in
what publishers, prizes, censors, and reading communities do to the text", the frame is
institutional. Both are legitimate; only one is Foucault.

The repair is not to swap in Genette and keep the analysis. It is to make the
institutional claim carry evidence of institutional acts, because those acts leave
public, checkable traces that a close reading does not.

A second tell of the same failure: the theory is listed and then never used. Four
numbered characteristics of a concept, followed by an analysis in which none of the
four appears, means the concept was decoration. Either drive the analysis through them
— showing which characteristic each finding instantiates — or delete the list.

### Trace the institution chain — four checkpoints that leave a public record

For a claim that a work's authorial authority was conferred by institutions rather than
by its style, walk the chain and record a date and a source at each step:

| Checkpoint | What to record | Where it survives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naming at issue | who chose the title, and whether the author later changed it | publisher's history, the editor's own interviews and memoirs |
| First public reception | which paper or journal staged the discussion, and when | that outlet's own archive, contemporary notices |
| Prize | which body awarded, which year, which cohort | the awarding association's own announcements |
| Canon / anthology | which selection admitted it, and its stated rationale | the series publisher's announcements |

The shape of the chain is itself the finding. A chain of **contest** (a title imposed by
an editor, reclaimed by the author within a year, a prize, a public debate in the same
season) and a chain of **consecration** (a journal appearance, a collection, a state
prize decades later, admission into a canon series) are two different institutional
mechanisms, and comparing their shapes is a thesis where comparing the two novels'
themes is not.

Two rules that keep this honest:

- **A chain is not a biography.** The claim is about what institutions did, not about
  what the author felt; every link must be an act with an actor and a date.
- **Say where the borrowed framework is too clean for the material.** Foucault built the
  author function on scientific and philosophical discourse, where the boundary between
  author function and author biography is sharp. In literature it is blurred, because a
  writer's own history is part of the authority the text carries. State that asymmetry in
  the body; leaving it implicit reads as applying a frame the paper does not understand.

- **The difference must be a mechanism, not a duration.** A comparison that reports only
  `hai năm` against `bốn thập kỷ` has measured the clock, not the process, and a referee
  says so in one line. Name what drives each chain. A **contest** chain advances by
  negation — each act overturns the one before it, and the overturning is itself what
  produces the position. A **consecration** chain advances by accretion — each act widens
  the previous one's scope without cancelling it. The falsifying question: could the short
  chain have produced the same authority by accretion, or the long one by negation? If
  either answer is yes, the axis is duration wearing a process label.

### An influence claim needs a documented chain

A comparison across centuries invites the sentence `nếu Han Kang đọc Ovid` — and that
sentence is the most severe defect a comparative-literature reviewer can find, because
it asserts reception without a single source. A shared image is not evidence of
influence; two texts using a tree is a coincidence until someone shows a text, an
interview, a letter, or a documented chain of reading.

The repair is not to soften the claim but to change its kind. Drop the influence
question and ask the functional one: `Nếu chim là dạng phổ biến nhất của Ovid, tại sao
Người ăn chay lại chọn cây?` — then answer from the texts, not from the author's
supposed reading. Write the disclaimer into the body rather than leaving it implicit:
`Bài viết không cho rằng Han Kang đọc Ovid, và cũng không có bằng chứng nào về ảnh
hưởng trực tiếp.`

The same rule applies to intention. `Sự thu hẹp này là một lựa chọn có chủ đích`
asserts design; without an interview or essay it becomes `có thể hiểu là một lựa chọn
có chủ đích, dù bài viết không có tuyên bố nào của tác giả để xác nhận`.

## 3b. Evidence of institutional acts needs a citation at every sentence

Literary-historical claims read as common knowledge and get written without a source,
because every sentence is checkable in seconds and none of them feels like an assertion.
The claims that escape are exactly the ones the argument stands on: who wrote a work and
when, who chose its title, which body awarded a prize in which year, which journal staged
a discussion on which date. A referee checking a thesis about institutions will look up
these dates, so each one carries its own citation at the sentence, not a shared one at
the paragraph's head.

The failure has a recognisable shape. A manuscript can hold sixteen references, pass a
two-way matching check between reference list and citations, and still carry a dozen
unsourced datable claims — because the matching check only asks whether cited works
appear in the list, never whether datable statements carry a citation. Run the second
check separately: split the body into sentences, and flag every sentence that contains a
year or a count and no author-year or numeric marker anywhere in it.

Two repairs, and the second is the one that matters:

- Attach the source to each datable sentence. Where a paragraph opens on a narrative
  citation (`Thái Hạo (2025) ...`) and the following sentences report its findings, the
  year still goes on the sentences that carry the numbers; a reader cannot tell which
  clause the paragraph head licenses.
- When a figure is reused in a later section, cite it again there. Restating `81,1%` in
  the discussion without its marker reads as a claim the paper discovered, not one it
  borrowed.

Find the source before writing the sentence, not after. Author-association biography
pages, the awarding body's own announcements, and the publishing house's or editor's
memoirs are the durable trace for a claim about who did what to a book; each is
quotable, and each names a date. Prefer them to secondary commentary, and prefer a
secondary source that names its own evidence over an encyclopedia entry that does not.

**An encyclopedia year is not a verified year.** Editions, prizes, and renamings fall in
different years in different sources, and the discrepancy is usually between the year of
the event and the year of the announcement. When two sources disagree, find the one
closest to the institution — the association that awarded, the house that printed — and
say which year you are using. The same care applies to a figure the manuscript quotes at
second hand: a count reported in one paper and reproduced in another can differ between
the registration number and the analysed sample, and the number the manuscript prints
must match the source it cites.

## 4. Hedge discipline in literary claims

Literary claims take a different hedge inventory from statistical claims, and the
forbidden upgrades are sharper because the evidence is interpretive.

| Weaker, correct | Forbidden upgrade |
| --- | --- |
| `gợi ý rằng` | `chứng minh rằng` |
| `có thể đọc như` | `là` |
| `cho thấy một xu hướng` | `cho thấy quy luật` |
| `tác giả có lẽ muốn` | `tác giả muốn` |
| `trong phạm vi 64 trường hợp` | `trong toàn bộ Metamorphoses` |

A p-value in a literary manuscript never licenses a claim about authorial intention.
`p = 0,039` supports `cây xuất hiện nhiều hơn trong nhóm bị theo đuổi`; it does not
support `Ovid cố ý chọn cây`. The statistical result and the interpretive claim are two
different registers, and the manuscript must not let the number carry the interpretation.

### A limitation and a conclusion must agree in strength

The commonest self-contradiction in a manuscript with statistics is a limitations
paragraph that disclaims a test and a conclusion that then leans on it. Measured case:
section 3 wrote `bài viết không dùng phép kiểm định này làm chứng cứ chính`, and section 7
wrote `Cây vì thế là dạng chuyên biệt cho một tình huống` — the `vì thế` restored the
causal reading the limitation had just retired. Both sentences are individually
reasonable, and together they tell a reviewer the author did not read their own caveat.

Sweep for the connective, not the claim: `vì thế`, `do đó`, `cho nên`, `therefore`
immediately after a hedged result. Replace with `dữ liệu gợi ý rằng` or `có thể là`.
The rule generalises: any sentence whose force exceeds the sentence that licensed it is
a defect, wherever the two sit in the document.

A wide confidence interval is a finding to report, not a number to bury in parentheses.
`CI 95% [1,14–18,56]` spans more than an order of magnitude; writing only `đạt ý nghĩa
thống kê ở mức 0,05` invites a reader to assume a stable effect. State the width and
what it means for the estimate. Likewise, post-hoc power computed without naming the
assumed effect size is not a design property — say which effect size it assumed, or
leave the number out.

## 5. False positives specific to literary prose

Do not flag these; each is correct Vietnamese that a data-paper rule misreads.

- **Short verbless sentences for emphasis.** `Trước cây là thịt.` Native rhythm, not a
  fragment error. Do not merge it into the next sentence.
- **Repetition of a key term across paragraphs.** `khát vọng`, `hình hài`, `biến hình`
  repeat by design. Replacing them with synonyms manufactures a distinction the argument
  does not make.
- **`chính` as an intensifier.** `chính sự đứng yên ấy là nội dung` is native emphasis,
  not padding.
- **Quoted Latin and its Vietnamese gloss.** Line references, italics, and the
  `Nghĩa là` lead-in are citation apparatus, not prose. En dashes inside
  `Met. 10.489–493` are notation.
- **A colon introducing a verbatim quotation.** `Ovid viết:` followed by a block quote
  is a legitimate colon; it is not the explanatory-colon pattern.
- **A colon inside a quoted English sentence.** When the block quotation itself contains
  a colon — Foucault's `...mode of being of discourse: the fact that...` — the scanner
  reports it as a defect in your prose. It is the source author's punctuation and cannot
  be edited.

### Adjudicate the colon scan against a density, not against zero

The `explanatory_colon` gate reports a count with a limit of 0, and the limit is wrong for
literary Vietnamese: the registry behind it allows roughly one per 1.000 words of body
prose. A 6.000-word manuscript therefore carries a budget of about five, and only the
surplus is a defect — the scan is a queue to triage, not a target to zero out.

The scan also runs over the whole file, so a share of every batch is out of scope before
you start: the title line, section headings, and the English abstract all count as hits
while none of them is body prose. Measured on one manuscript: 33 hits reduced to 2 by
recasting the body, and the 2 that survived were the legitimate-quotation cases above.
Chasing the remaining count to zero produces sentences worse than the ones it removed.

The highest-yield recast is the same in every instance: put a full stop where the colon
was and let the second half become its own sentence. `Câu trả lời nằm ở chuỗi thể chế:
tên do người khác đặt...` becomes `Câu trả lời nằm ở chuỗi thể chế. Tên do người khác
đặt...` — shorter, and it splits a sentence that was already running long.

## 6. Analytical sequence that survives review

1. Fix the axis of comparison before writing any analysis; if the axis needs a colon to
   state, it is not settled.
2. Establish what each text does structurally, separately, before comparing. A comparison
   that alternates between texts produces description, not argument.
3. Name the asymmetry rather than the similarity. `Cả hai đều X` is a survey finding;
   `A làm X bằng cách này, B làm X bằng cách khác, và khác biệt ấy là Y` is a thesis.
4. Read the concrete textual detail at the level of grammar and image, not only theme.
   Which noun is the subject, who narrates, which verb the text chooses — that is where
   a literary claim becomes checkable against the page.
5. Place the theoretical frame after the observation, never before it. A paragraph that
   opens with a theorist and then finds the text illustrates them is a literature review,
   not an argument.
6. Applications sections in a humanities paper state openings, not findings. A literature
   paper has evidence about two texts and none about classrooms, clinics, or social
   movements; a section claiming four practical applications converts an interpretation
   into an assertion nobody tested. Open the section by saying so — `Phần này không
   khẳng định rằng bài viết đã chứng minh được điều gì ngoài hai tác phẩm` — then give
   each opening its confidence level (`đây là một giả thuyết dạy học, chưa phải một kết
   quả nghiên cứu`), and keep every claim inside the texts' scope. A conditional
   opening section survives review; a confident one is the first thing a methodologist
   cuts.
7. When the central concept changes, re-derive every section written under the old one.
   A reframed thesis leaves residue the author will find before a reviewer does: the
   abstract and introduction get rewritten, and the body sections, the section headings,
   the conclusion, the limitations, and the title keep asserting the retired concept. The
   title is the most visible residue and the last thing checked: a manuscript whose thesis
   moved from theme to genre still carries a title naming the abandoned theme, and the
   author sees it on the first page before reading a word of the new argument. Sweep for the
   old term as a string before rebuilding the document, and treat a hit outside the
   rewritten sections as an unfinished rewrite rather than a synonym to smooth over.
   Rewriting only the sections the author named is the failure mode; the concept is
   load-bearing across the whole manuscript or it was never the thesis.

   **The retired concept survives longest as the thing being denied.** A sentence shaped
   as a refutation still names the old term, so `Khác biệt này không nằm ở chỗ Ovid có
   thần linh còn Han Kang thì không` reads as the new thesis while keeping the abandoned
   frame in the reader's ear. Grep for the old term regardless of negation: a hit inside
   `không phải ... mà`, `chứ không phải`, `rather than` is residue, not a concession.
   Replace the whole sentence with the positive claim (`Khác biệt là ở nhiệm vụ thể loại`,
   `Nó nằm ở việc trường ca cần một vật để chỉ vào`); a rewrite that keeps arguing against
   the old thesis has not moved, only turned around.

## 7. The author loop

**Decide, do not offer a menu.** When several architectures, titles, or framings are
viable, choose one, say in a single clause why, and build it. A numbered list of options
ending in `Anh chọn số nào?` transfers the decision back to the author, who asked for the
work rather than for a shortlist, and it costs a round trip per choice. Present
alternatives only when the choice turns on information the agent cannot obtain: a venue's
scope, an author's commitment to a theory, a source the agent has not been given.

**A named constraint overrides the pick.** When the author supplies the deciding
condition, rebuild around it instead of defending the earlier choice. `Hai tác phẩm ở hai
thời đại khác nhau` rules out any axis resting on individual authorial choice, because a
cross-era comparison explained by two temperaments is not an argument. Read such a
sentence as a specification, not as a remark, and re-derive the thesis from it.

**Calibrate register against published work in the target language.** When the author
calls a draft `dở` or `ngượng`, a synonym pass will not fix it, because the defect is
usually rhythm and rhythm is learned from models rather than from rules. Find three or
four published essays on the same work or the same kind of comparison in the target
language, read how they open and where they place their claims, then rebuild the passage
on that pattern. The tests in this file say what to avoid; a published paragraph shows
what to do instead. Report which sources you calibrated against, since the author is
judging whether the model was the right one.

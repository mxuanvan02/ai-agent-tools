# Vietnamese Literary and Comparative Prose

A word list catches the sentences you have already seen. A test catches the ones
you have not. This reference states the standard for Vietnamese prose about
literature as a set of tests that apply to any sentence, rather than a table of
phrases to replace.

The distinction matters because the same structural fault reappears under
unlimited surface forms. A writer told to avoid `nằm ở chỗ` writes `thuộc về
chỗ`, then `có gốc rễ ở`, and the fault survives every substitution. A writer
told that every sentence needs an actor and an observable predicate repairs the
fault wherever it appears, including in constructions no list contains.

Observed phrase inventories still have a place, but they belong to
[Vietnamese AI pattern registry](ai-pattern-vietnamese.md), which catalogues
what has already been seen. This file states what must be true.

## 1. The principle

A sentence of literary Vietnamese meets the standard when it carries three
things at once:

1. **A subject that does something.** Not a grammatical subject, but an actor
   whose action the reader can picture.
2. **A predicate naming an observable action or state.** Not a relation between
   abstractions, but something happening to someone or something.
3. **Information the reader did not have before the sentence.** Not a promise
   that information is coming.

A sentence missing one of the three is not badly worded. It is empty, and no
amount of rewording fills it. The repair is structural: restore the actor and
the action, or delete the sentence.

This is why literary Vietnamese reads as translated even when every word is
correct Vietnamese. Translation preserves the source's information
architecture, and English literary criticism is built on abstraction-first
sentences (`The difference lies in the fact that...`, `What is at stake here
is...`). Rendered clause by clause, those become Vietnamese sentences whose
subject is an abstraction and whose predicate is a relation. Grammatical,
idiomatic in vocabulary, hollow.

## 2. Sentence tests

Five tests. Apply all five to any sentence in body prose. None requires knowing
in advance whether the sentence is faulty.

### T1. Predicate test

**Ask:** what is the main verb, and what observable action or state does it
name?

**Fails when:** the main verb is `là`, `nằm ở`, `thuộc về`, `liên quan đến`,
`thể hiện`, `phản ánh`, or `đóng vai trò`, followed by an abstract noun. The
sentence asserts a relation between concepts instead of reporting something
that happens.

**Repair:** replace the relational verb with the verb of the actual event. If no
event comes to mind, the sentence has no content and should be deleted.

The repair is not "use a stronger verb." A stronger verb over an empty
proposition produces inflated prose. The repair is to find the event the
sentence was gesturing at.

### T2. Agency test

**Ask:** who does this?

**Fails when:** the answer is `điều này`, `việc ấy`, `quá trình`, `cách`, or
nothing at all. Nominalisation has removed the actor.

**Repair:** name the actor, or recast so the affected entity is the grammatical
subject with the agent in a `bởi` or `do` phrase. Vietnamese tolerates subject
ellipsis far more than English, so the actor may be omitted when recoverable
from the previous sentence; the test fails only when no actor is recoverable.

### T3. Deletion test

**Ask:** delete this clause. What does the sentence lose?

**Fails when:** nothing. The clause occupied space without carrying a claim.

**Repair:** delete it. Do not rewrite it into a better-sounding clause; a
clause that survives deletion was never carrying information.

This test catches the whole family of framing moves: announcing that a point
will be made, remarking that something is easy to miss, noting that a detail is
important. All fail deletion.

### T4. Back-translation test

**Ask:** translate the sentence into English. Does the result read like natural
English literary criticism?

**Fails when:** the English comes out suspiciously fluent, with the same clause
order and the same connectives as the Vietnamese. That fluency is the signature
of a calque: the Vietnamese was built on an English frame.

**Repair:** rebuild the sentence in Vietnamese order. In practice this means
moving the comment before the topic, dropping the connective that English
required, or splitting one English-shaped sentence into two Vietnamese ones.

Native Vietnamese literary prose and English literary criticism rarely share
clause order. When they do, one of them was copied.

### T5. Breath test

**Ask:** read the sentence aloud. Where do you run out of air?

**Fails when:** the sentence cannot be delivered in one breath, or the reader
must pause mid-clause to find the grammar.

**Repair:** place the full stop at the joint where the logical relation
changes, not where the noun phrase happens to end. A sentence of 55 words is a
revision target, but the count is a proxy; the real threshold is the breath.

Vietnamese academic prose favours shorter sentences than English academic
prose. A manuscript that reads as translated usually has correct grammar and
sentences one clause too long.

## 3. Voice standard

The tests in §2 keep prose from being empty. They do not make it literature.
Voice is a second standard, and it is what separates criticism that reads like a
report from criticism that reads like a writer thinking.

Eight properties, each stated as a test so it applies to any paragraph.

### V1. Open on a scene, not a summary

A paragraph about a literary work begins with something that happens in the
text, rendered in the text's own terms, and only then moves to the claim.

**Fails when:** the opening paragraph summarises the plot or the character's
biography in chronological order. A summary is information the reader could get
from the blurb, and it establishes nothing about why the work matters.

**Repair:** find the single image or action that carries the work's argument,
describe it in two or three sentences, then state what it shows.

### V2. The short sentence does the work

Vietnamese literary prose alternates long and short. The long sentence builds,
the short sentence lands. A paragraph made only of medium sentences has no
emphasis anywhere, and the reader cannot tell which sentence matters.

**Fails when:** every sentence in a paragraph runs 20 to 35 words. Nothing
lands, so nothing is memorable.

**Repair:** after building a claim across two or three clauses, close with a
sentence of six words or fewer that states the consequence. The short sentence
must carry a claim, not a transition.

### V3. Every paragraph has one sentence that survives alone

**Ask:** lift one sentence out of the paragraph. Does it still say something?

**Fails when:** no sentence in the paragraph can stand outside its neighbours,
because each one only modifies the previous.

**Repair:** the paragraph is doing description rather than argument. Find the
proposition the description was serving and state it as a sentence.

### V4. Verbs from the text, not from theory

**Ask:** where did this verb come from, the work or the framework?

**Fails when:** the verbs are `thể hiện`, `phản ánh`, `khẳng định`, `đặt vấn
đề`, `gợi mở`. These are the verbs of commentary, and they keep the sentence at
a distance from the page.

**Repair:** use the verb the work itself supplies. A character `cầu xin`,
`trườn lên`, `giữ chặt`, `luồn ống vào mũi`. The framework supplies the
question, not the vocabulary.

### V5. Images come from the work

**Fails when:** the figures of speech belong to the theory (`cấu trúc`, `mô
hình`, `trục`, `tầng`) rather than to the text (bark, sap, roots, a mouth held
shut, a hand on a shoulder).

**Repair:** describe with the work's own material. Theory may name the pattern
afterwards; it may not supply the picture.

### V6. Emotion through selection, not adjectives

**Ask:** does this paragraph tell the reader it is moving, or does it make the
reader move?

**Fails when:** the emotional work is done by adjectives: `ám ảnh`, `đau đớn`,
`bi thảm`, `lay động`. These claim an effect the prose has not produced.

**Repair:** delete the adjective and keep the detail that produced it. If no
detail produced it, the emotion was asserted rather than shown, and the sentence
goes.

### V7. Quote and interpret, never retell

**Fails when:** a paragraph narrates events in order. Criticism earns its space
by reading, not by summarising.

**Repair:** every narrative sentence must be doing interpretive work. If a plot
fact appears, it appears because the reading needs it, and it appears in the
smallest form that serves.

### V8. Close on weight, not on transition

**Fails when:** the last sentence of a paragraph or section announces what comes
next (`Tiếp theo, bài viết sẽ ...`, `Từ đó có thể thấy ...`).

**Repair:** close on the strongest proposition the paragraph has established, or
on the consequence that follows from it. The next section can open itself.

## 4. Title standard

A title meets the standard when it answers three questions. Punctuation is not
one of them.

1. **Does it name a concrete image or event in the text?** A tree, a meal, a
   look, a refusal. Not a concept.
2. **Does it state what the paper claims about that image?** The claim, not the
   topic.
3. **Can a reader who has not seen the abstract say what the paper argues about
   these two works?** If the title is narrower or wider than the argument, it
   fails.

Failure modes and their repairs:

- **Fails 1:** the title is a theory label. The paper has been named after its
  framework instead of its finding.
- **Fails 2:** the title names a shared topic. Two works both concern a theme,
  and the title stops there.
- **Fails 3:** the title promises one scope and the paper delivers another.

A colon or a spaced hyphen inside a Vietnamese title is a symptom, not the
disease. Both appear when the writer had two ideas and no syntax to join them,
which means the title is failing question 2: the first half names the topic and
the second half supplies the claim, because the writer could not compress both
into one phrase.

The repair is compression, not punctuation substitution. Find the phrase that
carries image and claim together, then let prepositions (`trong`, `của`, `và`)
do the joining work a colon would do in English.

## 5. Comparative argument standard

Three conditions. A comparative literature manuscript missing any of them is
read as arbitrary, and any quantitative appendix attached to it is read as
decoration.

1. **The comparison axis is stated before the analysis, in one sentence, and it
   is a relation rather than a shared theme.** `Both works concern the body` is
   a theme. `The form of narration decides whether the body can leave` is an
   axis, because it predicts where the two works will diverge.
2. **Both sides are described with the same conceptual vocabulary.** If one
   work is described in its own terms and the other in imported terms, the
   comparison is measuring the vocabulary, not the works.
3. **The conclusion states what the difference produces.** Not `the two works
   differ` but what follows from the difference for the question the paper
   asked.

A count or a p-value enters a comparative literary argument only after the axis
is fixed, because a number answers a question and cannot supply one.

## 6. Hedge standard for literary claims

The strength of the verb must match the kind of evidence behind it. This is a
matching rule, not a preference for caution.

| Evidence | Licensed verb strength |
| --- | --- |
| A quoted sentence from the work | `tác phẩm cho thấy`, `câu này đặt ...`, `ở đây ... xuất hiện` |
| An inference across several passages | `gợi ý`, `có thể đọc như`, `cho thấy một xu hướng` |
| A counted distribution | statements about the distribution only |
| A statistical test | statements about association within the sample |
| No evidence | `có lẽ`, or delete |

Two prohibitions follow directly:

- **No p-value licenses a claim about authorial intention.** A test can show
  that trees cluster in one group of cases. It cannot show that the poet chose
  trees for that reason.
- **No single passage licenses a claim about the work as a whole.** One
  quotation supports a reading of that passage; the scope marker
  (`trong trường hợp này`, `ở đoạn này`) is content, not padding.

A stack of hedges is also a fault, but a different one: it hides which evidence
supports which claim. Collapse the stack to one marker that names the evidence
type.

## 7. Register boundary

Literary Vietnamese is not a licence for ornament. The tests above are
structural, and ornament does not pass them.

What changes relative to data prose:

- **Short verbless sentences are available.** `Trước cây là thịt.` is native
  rhythm and passes T1 because the predicate is a real relation between
  concrete things.
- **Key terms repeat by design.** `khát vọng`, `hình hài`, `biến hình` recur
  because they are the analysis's objects. Repetition of a locked term is
  correct; introducing a synonym creates a second concept.
- **`chính` works as emphasis.** `chính sự đứng yên ấy là nội dung` passes T1:
  the predicate carries a claim.
- **Quotation apparatus is not prose.** Line numbers, italics, `Nghĩa là`
  lead-ins, and en dashes inside Latin line references are notation. The tests
  do not apply.

What does not change:

- Every sentence still needs an actor and an observable predicate (T1, T2).
- Every clause still has to survive deletion (T3).
- Every claim still has to match its evidence (hedge standard).

## 8. Audit procedure

Run the tests as an audit, not as a rewrite pass, so findings are reported
before prose changes.

1. **Scan for structural suspects.** List every sentence whose main verb is a
   relational verb (T1) or whose subject is a nominalisation (T2). This is a
   mechanical list, and it is where most failures sit.
2. **Apply T3 to every paragraph's first and last sentence.** Framing moves
   concentrate at paragraph boundaries.
3. **Apply T4 to any sentence that survived T1–T3 unchanged.** Those are the
   sentences where the calque hid behind correct-looking words.
4. **Apply T5 to every sentence over 55 words.** Report the breath point, not
   the word count.
5. **Report findings with the test number, not with a word list.** `T1: main
   verb "nằm ở" over an abstract subject` is a finding a writer can act on
   anywhere. `Avoid the phrase nằm ở chỗ` is a finding that stops at the
   sentence where it was noticed.

A scan that returns zero hits on T1 and T2 while the prose still reads
translated means the scan was run on words instead of on structure.

# Academic Document Architecture

A thesis, a journal article, a project report, and a course essay are the same shape at
different sizes. Learn the shape once and every genre is an instantiation of it.

## The governing principle: self-similar structure

Every level of an academic document repeats the same three beats:

**open (position) → body (develop) → close (settle and hand forward)**

A document opens, develops, and concludes. A part opens, develops, concludes. A chapter
opens, develops, concludes. A section does the same at smaller scale. This is why a
reader who has understood one chapter can navigate an unfamiliar document: the pattern
is predictable, and only the content is new.

The failure mode is a level that skips a beat. A chapter with no opening makes the
reader reconstruct why it exists; a chapter with no closing leaves the next chapter
unmotivated; a section with no closing leaves a list of facts that never resolves into
a position. These are structural defects, not style preferences, and they are visible
at any scale.

## The three parts

| Part | Question it answers | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| Mở đầu / Introduction | why this, and what exactly | problem, gap, objective, scope |
| Nội dung / Body | how, and what came out | chapters or IMRaD sections |
| Kết luận / Conclusion | how far did it get | answers, limits, next step |

The three parts are not three topics. They are one argument in three tenses: what is
unresolved (future), what was done (past), what now stands (present perfect). A body
chapter that re-argues the introduction, or a conclusion that introduces new evidence,
breaks the tense structure and the document stops being one argument.

## Level by level

| Level | Opens with | Body | Closes with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole document | abstract or Mở đầu | parts | Kết luận |
| Part | one short paragraph naming the part's function | chapters | a part may have no closing; if it does, it states what the part settled |
| Chapter | **giới thiệu chung** — 1 paragraph: what this chapter does, why the previous chapter left it necessary, what it will hand on | sections | **tiểu kết chương** — what was settled, what that now forces |
| Section | one sentence naming the section's object | paragraphs or subsections | one sentence stating the position reached |
| Paragraph | topic sentence | 3–5 sentences of development | closing sentence that resolves or bridges |

Two consequences worth stating explicitly, because both are commonly violated:

**The chapter opening is not a table of contents.** `Chương này gồm ba phần: …` names
structure the reader can already see. The opening must state *function* — what this
chapter establishes that nothing before it established.

**The chapter closing is not a summary.** A tiểu kết that repeats section titles is
duplication. It must state what is now settled and what obligation that places on the
next chapter. If the last sentence could be deleted without changing what the next
chapter must do, the closing has not done its job.

## Chapters follow the objectives, one to one

This is the rule that makes a document defensible in a viva. If Mở đầu states *n*
specific objectives, the body has *n* chapters, in the same order. The mapping is
checkable in both directions:

- forward: every objective has a chapter that answers it;
- backward: every chapter answers an objective stated in Mở đầu.

A chapter that answers no stated objective is either an extra contribution (then the
objective list is incomplete) or padding. An objective with no chapter is a promise the
document does not keep. Both are found by a committee in minutes, and both are cheap to
fix before submission and expensive to fix after.

## Sentence-level structure: the part that is actually writable

Word counts are a symptom; sentence structure is the cause. Each unit below has a
fixed move order, and the moves are what you write against — the count follows.

**Abstract (journal article, or a thesis that carries a prose abstract).** Six moves,
one to two sentences each: context or problem → gap → objective → method or material →
principal result with its quantity → bounded conclusion. No citation, no claim absent
from the body, and the claim boundary stated inside the abstract when the result is
mechanism-level.

**Mở đầu.** Territory → what is known and where it stops → the gap stated as something
unresolved rather than something under-studied → objective → scope → contribution as a
bounded claim. The gap is the hinge: everything before it earns the right to state it,
everything after it answers it.

**Chapter opening (giới thiệu chung).** Three beats in one paragraph: what this chapter
does; why the previous chapter made it necessary; what it hands to the next.

**Chapter closing (tiểu kết).** Two beats: what is now established; what that obliges.

**Kết luận.** Answer the objectives **in the order they were stated**, then the
applicability limit, then the warranted next step. No new claim, no new number, no
widening of scope.

## Same shape, different instantiation

| Genre | Mở đầu | Nội dung | Kết luận |
|---|---|---|---|
| Journal article | Introduction (funnel to gap) | Methods, Results, Discussion | Conclusion inside Discussion, or separate |
| Master's thesis / dissertation | Mở đầu, usually 5–7 numbered sections | chapters mapped to objectives | Kết luận, often 2 unnumbered sections |
| Project report (đề tài) | Mở đầu + a reporting form (objectives, novelty, products) | chapters | Kết luận + deliverables |
| Undergraduate khóa luận | shorter Mở đầu, same moves | 2–3 chapters | Kết luận, brief |
| Course essay / tiểu luận | 1–2 paragraphs, problem and aim | 2–3 numbered sections | 1 paragraph |

The genre changes the *number* of levels and the *reporting artifacts* attached to
them. It does not change the three beats, and it does not change the objective-to-chapter
mapping. A course essay that states one aim and then writes three unrelated sections has
the same defect as a dissertation that does, at a smaller scale.

## What scale actually looks like (one measured instance)

Measured from a completed Vietnamese project report (4 chapters, ~90 pages): Mở đầu ≈ 4 000 words across six sections, of which the literature review is
**half**; the objective statement is **one sentence**; each objective maps to exactly
one chapter; contribution chapters run roughly 3 000 words of method against 2 500–3 700
of results; every chapter closes with a 160–280-word tiểu kết; Kết luận is 537 + 339
words in two unnumbered sections.

Use those as a sanity band, not a target. A section at three times or one fifth of the
band is a signal that something structural is off — usually that a chapter is carrying
two objectives, or that a section is doing a chapter's work.

## Checks, at any scale

1. Does every level have all three beats, or does one skip its opening or closing?
2. Does the number of body chapters equal the number of stated objectives, in order?
3. Does each chapter opening state function rather than structure?
4. Does each chapter closing state an obligation rather than a summary?
5. Does the abstract carry all six moves, with a quantity in the result move?
6. Does Kết luận answer in the stated order, adding nothing new?

A document can pass every wording check and fail all six. That is why architecture is
checked before prose.
